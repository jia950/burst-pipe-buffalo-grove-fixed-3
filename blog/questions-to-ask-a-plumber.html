Clear questions make it easier to compare proposals and understand the work before it begins.

When to contact a professional

If the source is unclear, the leak continues, or pipe access requires opening walls or floors, arrange an assessment from an appropriately qualified plumbing professional. Do not take risks around electrical hazards, structural damage, or contaminated water.

Questions About Credentials and Estimates

Before you agree to any work, ask the provider for their name, company and license number. In Illinois, plumbers are licensed through the Illinois Department of Public Health, so a license number is something you can verify. Ask whether the estimate is written, what it includes, what is excluded and how extra work would be priced. Confirm the arrival window and whether there is a service fee. Remember that we are a referral website, so credentials, pricing and availability are always confirmed directly with the independent provider. If you are dealing with an urgent problem, our emergency plumbing help page lists what to have ready.

Questions About the Repair Itself

Ask what caused the problem, whether other pipes are at risk and what the repair involves. For a burst or leaking line, ask whether the plumber will repair the section or recommend pipe repair and replacement. Ask how long the work will take, whether walls or floors need to be opened and who repairs them afterward. Ask what warranty, if any, covers the work. If water has damaged the home, ask about drying and read our water damage cleanup guidance. A good provider welcomes these questions. Red Flags to Watch For

Be careful if a provider refuses to give a license number, will not provide a written estimate, pressures you to decide on the spot or asks for full payment in cash before any work. Be wary of prices that seem far too low, because extra charges often appear later. A reliable plumber explains the problem, shows you the damaged part when possible and answers questions patiently.
