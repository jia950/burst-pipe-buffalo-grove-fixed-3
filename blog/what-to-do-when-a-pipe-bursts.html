What to Do When a Pipe Bursts at Home

A burst pipe can be stressful. These practical first steps can help you protect people and reduce additional water damage while arranging professional assistance.

A burst pipe can be stressful. These practical first steps can help you protect people and reduce additional water damage while arranging professional assistance.

Stay safe first. Keep clear of water near electrical outlets or equipment.

If the main shutoff valve is safely accessible, turn off the water supply. Do not force a stuck valve.

Move belongings away from water if it is safe to do so. Take photos for your records.

Contact a local plumbing professional and explain where the pipe is leaking and whether water is still flowing.

After the leak is controlled, assess wet materials and arrange appropriate drying.

When to contact a professional

If the source is unclear, the leak continues, or pipe access requires opening walls or floors, arrange an assessment from an appropriately qualified plumbing professional. Do not take risks around electrical hazards, structural damage, or contaminated water.

What Happens After the Water Is Off

Once the main valve is closed, open a low faucet to drain the remaining water and, if it is safe, move belongings away from wet areas. A plumber will locate the break, confirm whether the split is limited to one section and decide between a repair and a replacement. Small splits in copper or PEX can often be repaired by installing a new section of pipe, while corroded or repeatedly failing runs may call for pipe repair and replacement. When you call for burst pipe repair in Buffalo Grove, IL, describe where the water appeared, what the pipes are made of and what you have already done, so the provider can prepare the right parts and tools.

Cleanup and Prevention After a Burst Pipe

After the repair, the structure still needs drying. Follow our water damage cleanup guidance and remove soaked carpet pad and insulation quickly. To prevent another burst, insulate pipes in cold spaces, learn how to prevent frozen pipes in winter and make sure everyone in the household knows where the main water shutoff valve is. If the burst happened in an exterior wall or a garage, ask the plumber to check the nearby pipes as well, because pipe next to a break is often weakened by the same cold or corrosion.

Common Mistakes After a Pipe Bursts

The most common mistake is waiting too long to shut off the water. Others include walking through standing water near outlets, ignoring moisture hidden in walls and hiring someone without checking credentials. Do not rely on tape or a temporary clamp for long, and do not assume the problem is over when the water stops. Follow up with drying, as described in our water damage cleanup guidance, and ask the plumber to check nearby pipes. For urgent help, see emergency plumbing help.
