Where Small Leaks Hide

Small leaks like to hide in places you rarely look: under sinks, behind toilets, around the water heater, inside wall cavities and along basement ceilings. Check supply lines and shutoff valves under sinks for moisture, look for stains below second-floor bathrooms and listen for dripping or hissing when the house is quiet. A simple meter test also helps: turn off every tap and appliance, note the reading and check it again after an hour. If it moved, water is going somewhere. When you suspect a leak you cannot see, ask about pipe leak repair in Buffalo Grove, IL so a plumber can locate it with moisture checks and pressure tests.

Why Fixing a Small Leak Early Pays Off

A leak that drips only a little can still soak drywall, insulation and subflooring, and wet materials can grow mold within 24 to 48 hours, according to the EPA. Early repairs are usually simpler and cheaper than repairing the damage a long-running leak leaves behind. If the leak may be under a concrete floor, read about the signs of a slab leak. If water has already spread, our water damage cleanup guidance explains the steps to dry the area. Residents of Arlington Heights and Wheeling with older plumbing should pay special attention to fittings and pinhole leaks, which often start small and spread over time.

A Simple Monthly Leak Check

Once a month, check under every sink, around the toilet bases, behind the washing machine and near the water heater for moisture or stains. Look at your ceilings and basement walls, and compare your water bill with previous months. Test your shutoff valves, and make sure you know where the main water shutoff valve is. If you find a problem, close the nearest valve, photograph the area and call for pipe leak repair. Five minutes a month can prevent a major repair.
