How to Find Your Main Water Shutoff Valve

Knowing where the main water shutoff is before an emergency can save time. Valve locations vary by home and plumbing layout.

Knowing where the main water shutoff is before an emergency can save time. Valve locations vary by home and plumbing layout.

Look in likely utility areas such as a basement, crawl space, utility room, or near where the water line enters the home.

If you are unsure which valve controls the supply, consult a plumber rather than guessing during an emergency.

Keep access to the valve clear and tell household members where it is.

Never enter a flooded area or approach electrical equipment to reach a valve.

When to contact a professional

If the source is unclear, the leak continues, or pipe access requires opening walls or floors, arrange an assessment from an appropriately qualified plumbing professional. Do not take risks around electrical hazards, structural damage, or contaminated water.

Common Places to Find the Main Shutoff Valve in Buffalo Grove Homes

Most homes in the Buffalo Grove area have a basement, and the main shutoff is usually on the wall or floor where the water line enters, close to the water meter and often near the water heater. In homes without a full basement, look in a utility room, a closet near the water heater or a crawl space. Follow the main line from the meter and look for a lever handle or a round wheel on the pipe. Once you find it, tag the valve and take a photo, so everyone in the home can locate it quickly. If you still cannot find it, our main water shutoff valve help page explains what to ask a plumber.

Test the Valve Before You Need It

A valve that has not moved in years can seize. In a calm moment, turn the valve off and on once to confirm that it works and that it does not leak at the handle. A lever valve moves a quarter turn, while a round gate valve turns clockwise to close. Never force a stuck valve, because it can break and make the problem worse. If it will not turn, call for emergency plumbing help or ask a plumber to replace it before winter. Knowing your shutoff is the single best way to limit damage if you ever need burst pipe repair, and it pairs well with the steps in our guide on what to do when a pipe bursts. Homeowners in Buffalo Grove and Long Grove may also have outdoor shutoffs for hose lines, which are worth labeling too.

What to Do If the Valve Is Stuck or Leaking

If the valve will not turn, do not force it. A stuck gate valve can break, and a valve that leaks at the handle may be failing. Try the other shutoff points in your home, such as the valve under the affected fixture, and call for emergency plumbing help if water is flowing. A plumber can repair or replace a worn valve, and some homeowners choose to upgrade an old gate valve to a lever-style ball valve that is easier to use in an emergency. If the valve is part of aging plumbing, ask about pipe repair and replacement at the same visit, so the work is done once.
