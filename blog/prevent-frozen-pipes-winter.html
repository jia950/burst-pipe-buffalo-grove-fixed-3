How to Help Prevent Frozen Pipes in Winter

Cold weather can put exposed or poorly insulated plumbing at risk. Prevention depends on the property and the severity of the weather.

A Winter Pipe Protection Checklist

Frozen pipes are one of the most common causes of winter plumbing emergencies in northern Illinois. Walk through the house in the fall and check the places pipes are most exposed: exterior walls, attached garages, crawl spaces, attic runs and rooms over unheated spaces. Add insulation where pipes are bare, seal drafts around pipes where they enter the house and disconnect garden hoses. During a hard freeze, keep cabinet doors under sinks open, let a faucet drip and keep the thermostat steady, even if you are away. If a Pipe Freezes Anyway

If water slows to a trickle or stops, open the faucet and warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer or warm towels, working from the faucet back toward the frozen spot. Never use an open flame. If you see a crack, a bulge or water, close the main valve and call for frozen pipe repair. After the thaw, check walls and ceilings for hidden leaks over the next day or two, because a split pipe may only leak once the ice melts. If water did get out, follow our water damage cleanup guidance and dry the area quickly to limit mold. Homeowners in Vernon Hills, Long Grove and Riverwoods with long pipe runs in garages and outbuildings should check those areas first.

Planning for a Long Cold Snap or a Vacation

If you travel in winter, keep the thermostat set to a safe temperature, no lower than about 55 degrees, and ask someone to check the house. For long absences, some homeowners shut off the main water and drain the lines, which a plumber can explain. Before a forecasted deep freeze, check garages and crawl spaces, close foundation vents and cover exposed pipes. If a pipe does burst, know your main water shutoff valve and call for emergency plumbing help.
