What to Do After a Plumbing Leak Is Stopped

Stopping the leak is only the first step. Materials may remain wet after the visible water is gone.

Stopping the leak is only the first step. Materials may remain wet after the visible water is gone.

Keep people away from unsafe electrical or structural areas.

Document visible damage and contact your insurer if appropriate.

Arrange suitable drying and moisture assessment; concealed moisture can remain in walls or flooring.

Ask qualified restoration professionals about affected materials and safe cleanup methods.

When to contact a professional

If the source is unclear, the leak continues, or pipe access requires opening walls or floors, arrange an assessment from an appropriately qualified plumbing professional. Do not take risks around electrical hazards, structural damage, or contaminated water.

Drying and Cleanup Basics After a Plumbing Leak

Once the leak is repaired, the next job is drying. Remove standing water, lift wet items off floors and run fans and a dehumidifier. Remove carpet pad and soaked insulation that cannot dry, and open cabinet doors so air reaches the walls behind. Keep going until walls and floors are dry all the way through, not just on the surface. Preventing the Next Leak

After any leak, ask the plumber what caused it and whether other pipes are at risk. A single corroded section may be a sign that the rest of the run is aging, in which case pipe repair and replacement may be worth discussing. Replace worn washing machine and dishwasher hoses, check shutoff valves under sinks and learn where your main water shutoff valve is. If the leak was caused by freezing, read how to prevent frozen pipes in winter. Homeowners in Buffalo Grove and Lincolnshire with finished basements should also check there regularly, since small leaks can run unnoticed for days.

When to Call a Professional After a Leak

Call a professional if the leak ran for more than a few hours, carpet is soaked, drywall is sagging, floors are buckling or you can smell mold. Larger losses need equipment and moisture readings that most homeowners do not have. Start with emergency plumbing help if water is still flowing, then arrange drying and cleanup. The EPA also publishes a brief guide to mold, moisture and your home that explains when mold growth needs professional help.
