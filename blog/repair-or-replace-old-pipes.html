How Plumbers Decide Between Repair and Replacement

Plumbers usually look at four things: the age and material of the pipe, how many leaks it has had, how bad the corrosion is and how hard the pipe is to reach. An isolated failure in an otherwise healthy line can often be fixed with a new section. Repeated leaks, rusty water, low pressure or visible corrosion usually point toward replacing a longer run. Ask for a written scope that shows what will be opened, what will be replaced and who restores walls and floors. Common Pipe Materials in Older Homes

Older homes may have galvanized steel, copper, cast iron drains or early plastic piping, and each ages differently. Galvanized pipe can corrode from the inside and restrict flow, copper can develop pinhole leaks and some older plastics become brittle. If you are not sure what you have, ask a plumber to identify it during a visit. When a leak keeps coming back, compare the cost of another repair with the cost of replacing the worn section, and ask about pipe leak repair options for isolated problems. If a pipe has already burst, start with burst pipe repair. Planning a Pipe Replacement Project

Replacing pipe can mean water shutoffs, opened walls and several days of work. Ask how long the water will be off, which rooms will be affected, whether permits are needed and who repairs drywall and paint afterward. Make sure you know where your main water shutoff valve is, since a plumber may ask you to confirm it works before starting. After the work, ask for a written summary of what was replaced and what was left in place, so you know which pipes may need attention later.
